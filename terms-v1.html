2 What Etmar is — and what it is not

(1) Etmar is a logbook, a racing and trim aid and an addition on board recreational boats. Etmar is not navigation equipment, not approved navigation or safety equipment, and not intended for commercial shipping.

(2) Etmar is no substitute for official nautical charts, a chartplotter, a depth sounder, a lookout, a watch, the official marine weather forecast or good seamanship. The emergency functions are no substitute for a marine radio or an emergency call: Etmar does not send a distress call.

(3) Map. The map is based on OpenStreetMap: seamarks are community data, the depth contours come from a model on a roughly 100 m grid (EMODnet). Neither is an official source, neither is corrected from Notices to Mariners, and neither is a basis for navigation. Dashed depth contours marked ≈ are approximate. Go-To and routes show bearing and distance to points you set; they check neither land nor depth nor obstacles.

(4) Wind and weather. Wind from open sources comes from weather stations and from a weather model. It arrives with a delay, applies to the station's location or to a coarse grid, and is neither a marine weather forecast nor a gale warning.

(5) Instruments. Etmar shows values from the onboard network (NMEA) as they arrive from the gateway. Etmar cannot tell whether a sensor is miscalibrated.

(6) Radio procedure cards. The SOS view is a reading aid for the radio call. The call goes out over your marine radio (VHF channel 16, DSC) or by phone, not through Etmar.

3 Limits of the alarms and emergency functions

(1) Anchor watch, depth alarm, wind alarm, AIS warning and MOB help — they are no substitute for a watch, a lookout or a depth sounder. They can fail: battery flat, iOS closes the app, no GPS, onboard network down, device out of earshot. The Provider does not promise that an alarm will go off in every situation. In addition, each function has the following limits.

(2) Anchor watch. It watches with the iPhone's GPS and alerts you when the boat leaves the circle you set around the anchor; it allows for GPS accuracy. Limits:

  1. With a flat battery there is no alarm.
  2. If iOS closes the app, the watch may stop. If you swipe Etmar away in the app switcher, it no longer watches.
  3. It needs location access “Always” and “Precise Location”. If no usable position arrives for two minutes, it shows “WATCH BLIND” instead of watching.
  4. With the device locked you only hear the alarm if you allow notifications for Etmar. If the iPhone is on silent, the sound only plays with “Critical Alerts” allowed; otherwise you only get the vibration.

(3) Depth alarm. It runs while the anchor is set and needs a depth sounder on the onboard network. Limits:

  1. It works with the depth below the transducer, not below the keel. It does not apply a keel offset set on the onboard network.
  2. It measures the depth now; it does not predict falling water.
  3. If no sounding arrives for a minute, it shows “no depth” — then it is not watching.

(4) Wind alarm. It runs while the anchor is set and compares the wind with the wind when you anchored (shifts and increase). Limits:

  1. The increase needs a wind sensor on the onboard network. Without a wind sensor it only detects a shift, from the way the boat lies to the anchor. In a current that shows the current, not the wind; closer than 15 m to the anchor the lie is unclear.
  2. It assesses averages over three minutes. It does not report a single gust.
  3. If neither wind nor a usable lie arrives for a minute, it shows “no wind” — then it is not watching.
  4. It is not a gale warning.

(5) AIS warning. It needs AIS data from the onboard network and warns when a vessel, based on its reported position and motion, will come closer than the threshold you set (CPA/TCPA). Limits:

  1. It only sees vessels that transmit AIS. Many craft do not transmit AIS or have it switched off.
  2. If AIS reception drops out, Etmar does not report it. Only before the first AIS signal has arrived does the layers menu show “AIS on — no targets received yet”.
  3. It stops calculating for a vessel from which no new position has arrived for three minutes.
  4. Without a current course of your own, it calculates as if your boat were stationary.
  5. It is no substitute for a lookout or radar. The Collision Regulations apply.

(6) MOB. When someone presses and holds the MOB button, it records position and time and shows bearing and distance to that point. Limits:

  1. MOB does not watch. Etmar does not detect by itself that someone has gone overboard. The alarm only comes when someone on board holds the button.
  2. Etmar does not send a distress call.
  3. The position is as accurate as the GPS at the moment of triggering. If the last position is too old, Etmar marks the point as provisional or approximate. Etmar does not account for the drift of the person in the water.

(7) Alarm and emergency functions are always free and remain usable while consent to these terms or to a new text version is pending (section 13).
