Privacy Policy

Data controller: Morten Göpfert, Spaltsberg 7, 23730 Neustadt in Holstein, Germany, email: support@etmar.app

Principle

Etmar is an offline-first app. All content (boats, voyages, logbook entries, trim data, photos, track points) is stored locally on your device. There is no server of the provider’s own, no account, no advertising, no tracking and no analytics services. The app does not pass your content on to third parties. Some features do, however, query outside services and send individual details along — which ones, where to and when is set out in the following sections. With each of these requests the service learns your IP address, as when calling up any web page; the app adds no device or user identifier.

iCloud synchronisation

Only if you turn it on — at first launch or under Settings → Data & privacy → “iCloud backup” — does the app use iCloud (CloudKit, private container) for backup and synchronisation between your devices. The data is held in your personal iCloud account with Apple; the provider of this app has no access to it. Apple’s privacy policy applies. With iCloud backup off, your data stays on the device; anything you uploaded earlier remains in your iCloud until you delete it there.

Location data

The app records your GPS position (in the background too, if you wish) in order to create logbook entries and record voyage tracks. The anchor alarm and the race countdown also work in the background. The app stores your track and positions on your device and — if you turn on iCloud backup — in your private iCloud. All that leaves the device is individual coordinates for place names (to Apple, see “Place names”) and map views or coarse areas for the map and wind (see “Map data” and “Wind from open sources”). You can withdraw the permission at any time in the iOS settings.

Photos and camera

For every photo you add in the app, it stores a reduced copy (up to 1,500 pixels; for trim, sail and equipment photos up to 2,400 pixels) and a small preview in its own database — so the picture appears sharp even without a connection and without access to your library. These copies are part of your data: they go into iCloud backup if you have turned it on, and into every full backup. The app does not keep the location details (EXIF/GPS) of the original. Photos you take with the camera in the app are also saved to your photo library; iOS asks once for permission to add photos. The app only reads from your library through the iOS photo picker, i.e. only the pictures you choose there.

Map data

The base map comes from OpenFreeMap (delivered via Cloudflare), seamarks and harbours from the Overpass servers of the OpenStreetMap community (overpass-api.de, or overpass.kumi.systems as a fallback), depth contours from EMODnet Bathymetry. In doing so the requested map view and your IP address go to these services: to all three when you download an offline area, and to OpenFreeMap also when the map fetches the view you are looking at while “Load map online” is on (on by default; Settings → Data & privacy). If the map follows your boat, these requests roughly reveal where you are. The app does not transmit voyages, entries or the track. If you save your area as an offline package, these requests do not happen at sea. The map in the destination search, which shows places nearby, loads its view from Apple (MapKit).

Place names

The app determines place names via Apple (MapKit): when you cast off, for your current position (name of the voyage and of the departure entry); in the logbook for your berth if it is not in a saved harbour; when you create a destination, waypoint or offline area; for points you tap on the map; and for the marks of a course in the course library. Each time, that single coordinate goes to Apple. If you use the place search, the search text you enter goes to Apple to find matching places. Without a connection the name stays empty. Apple’s privacy policy applies.

Onboard network (NMEA 2000 and NMEA 0183)

If you connect the app to a gateway on board, instrument data (wind, depth, heading, position, AIS) is received exclusively on the local network and processed locally. AIS messages contain the name, identifier (MMSI) and position of other vessels; the app shows them on the map and uses them for collision warnings. You start a raw recording of the data stream yourself; in a race’s pre-start the app also starts it of its own accord when a gateway is connected, so that the analysis afterwards is complete. These files contain everything that came over the onboard network, including other vessels’ AIS messages. They stay on the device until you share them or delete them under Settings → NMEA connection → Advanced.

Wind from open sources

Without a wind sensor the app fetches wind from coastal stations of the German weather service (DWD) and the Danish DMI, in Sweden from stations of the SMHI, in the US from buoys and coastal stations of the National Data Buoy Center (NDBC) of the US agency NOAA, otherwise from NOAA’s GFS weather model — for three purposes: for “Wind now” on the chart and the wind suggestion in a logbook entry it requests the current value every 15 minutes when online (held in memory only, never stored), and when you open the “Wind now” sheet also the model value at that spot for comparison; after a voyage without wind from the onboard network it fetches the hourly wind of that voyage the next time it opens with a connection, never in the background, and stores it with the voyage; and if you have switched on the “Wind (model)” or “Wind (stations)” layer on the map, it fetches, while the map is open, the model field at most once per hour and map view or every 15 minutes the values of up to ten stations in view, respectively (held in memory only). The device picks the station itself from a built-in list. Only the station ID goes to the DWD and the NDBC, the ID and whether the last hour, the last day or the last months are needed to the SMHI, the ID and time span to the DMI, and, for the model, the whole one-degree cell (1° × 1°, roughly 110 × 65 km) plus the hour to NOAA — for the layer, instead of the cell, the bounds of the map view rounded to quarter degrees, more coarsely when zoomed far out (at most 64° × 64°) — along with your IP address. That reveals the sailing area or the area you are looking at, not your exact position; the app sends no coordinates in the process. The hourly values of a longer voyage (the nearest station or the cell for each hour) do, however, reveal its rough route. In addition there is the forecast before a race: if you open “Wind against the course” for a race with a course, or tap “Fetch again” there, the app fetches the GFS forecast for the hours of the race; each request sends NOAA the cells around the course marks (whole degrees; 1° × 1° for a course within one cell) and the hour. The forecast stays on the device as a file, not in the backup, until it is deleted at the next request once its last hour is two days past. In the race demo the app — likewise only with “Wind from open sources” and a connection, every 15 minutes while the demo is in front — fetches the current wind of the nearest station and the GFS forecast for the demo course in Neustadt Bay (cell 54–55° N, 10–11° E), not for your position; the same details as above go out, for this fixed area. The demo forecast is deleted when the demo ends, or like any other forecast if the app is closed during the demo. The app no longer queries Apple Weather. You can switch all of it off under Settings → Data & privacy → “Wind from open sources” (on by default).

Backing up and sharing

Full backups, GPX/CSV export, PDF reports and sharing individual destinations, courses or routes create files on your device. A full backup contains all content including photos, a report also crew names and photos, a GPX track also the notes of the entries. Where the files go is entirely your decision — the app sends nothing of its own accord.

Submissions to Etmar

Courses and harbour entries can be sent to the provider voluntarily so that they ship with a future version. For this the app prepares an email to submissions@etmar.app; it is only sent by you. What is transmitted is only the content listed in the submission sheet (name, coordinates, course order, dates, notes — for harbour entries also the selected photos), plus your sender address through the sending of the mail itself. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) and the carrying out of the transmission you initiated. The provider checks the submission by hand and includes it in the app where appropriate; only the course or harbour data is ever published, never your email address or your name — unless you expressly name a club or organiser as the source. The correspondence is deleted as soon as it is no longer needed for queries, at the latest after twelve months. You can withdraw your consent at any time without any particular form; content already shipped is then removed with the next version. Only send in content to which you hold the rights — photos in particular — and no images in which people are identifiable.

Feedback and support

If you write via “Feedback” in the app or directly to support@etmar.app, the provider receives your email address and your text, and only if you leave “Attach technical details” on, also the app version, device type, iOS version, language, tier and whether the demo is running. The app only prepares the email; you send it. The legal basis is handling your request (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). The emails are deleted as soon as they are no longer needed for the request, at the latest after twelve months.

Crash reports and beta test

The app itself sends no crash or usage reports. If you have turned on sharing with app developers in the iOS settings, Apple provides the provider with crash reports that are not linked to you. If you take part in the beta test via TestFlight, Apple gives the provider your name and email address if you were invited by email, as well as feedback, screenshots and crash reports you send via TestFlight. Apple’s terms apply.

Purchases and subscriptions

The subscription is handled entirely through the App Store (Apple). The app neither receives nor stores payment data; it only learns whether a subscription is active. Apple’s privacy policy applies to the purchase process.

Website etmar.app

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., USA). When you visit it, GitHub processes your IP address and technical details of your browser in server logs in order to deliver the pages and protect against abuse. The website sets no cookies, loads no third-party fonts or scripts and uses no analytics services. The legal basis is the legitimate interest in secure delivery (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Legal bases

What the app stores and calculates on your device stays with you. The requests to outside services (map, place names, wind and forecast) serve the features you use; the legal basis is providing these features (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and the legitimate interest in a working app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). For submissions your consent applies (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). The services queried process your IP address under their own terms.

Transfers to third countries

Apple (iCloud, MapKit, App Store, TestFlight), Cloudflare (map delivery), GitHub (website) and the US weather agency NOAA (GFS weather model, NDBC buoys) also process data in the USA. For users in the EU, Apple Distribution International Ltd. (Ireland) is responsible at Apple; Apple bases the transfer to Apple Inc. in the USA on the EU Commission’s standard contractual clauses. Cloudflare and GitHub are certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, for which the EU Commission has found an adequate level of data protection. NOAA is a US government agency and not certified; all it receives is your IP address, a one-degree cell, a map view or a station ID and the hour — and only while “Wind from open sources” is on. The other services named (Overpass, EMODnet, DWD, DMI, SMHI) are based in the EU.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and to object to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21). You can withdraw consent at any time with effect for the future. As your content is held by you, you can inspect and export it yourself at any time (full backup, GPX, CSV) and remove it completely by deleting the app or the iCloud data (iOS settings → your name → iCloud → Manage storage → Etmar). For submissions and emails, contact the email address given in the legal notice. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example the Unabhängiges Landeszentrum für Datenschutz Schleswig-Holstein in Kiel.
