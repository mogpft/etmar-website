2 What Etmar is — and what it is not

(1) Etmar is a voyage journal, a racing and trim aid and an app for recording, analysing and managing, for recreational boats. Etmar is not navigation equipment, not approved navigation or safety equipment, and not intended for commercial shipping. While Etmar is recording a voyage, it shows measured values and the pre-start timer but no map and no bearing to a place.

(2) Etmar is no substitute for official nautical charts, a chartplotter, a depth sounder, a lookout, a watch, the official marine weather forecast or good seamanship. The emergency functions are no substitute for a marine radio or an emergency call: Etmar does not send a distress call.

(3) Map. Etmar shows a map only while it is not recording a voyage: for planning beforehand and for looking back afterwards. It does not show your boat's position. The map is based on OpenStreetMap. Etmar offers seamarks only as a selection aid in the course editor (“Load seamarks”): they are community data, not an official source, not corrected from Notices to Mariners, and for orientation only, not for navigation. Etmar does not show water depths, neither on a map nor in a report. Etmar does not check any course against depth, land or obstructions. To the extent Etmar offers a practice course, Etmar lays it from the position and length you choose and the wind; its marks are virtual, their position is not checked. For depths, land and obstructions, the official nautical chart applies. Etmar shows no bearing and no distance to a place. The exception is the distance to a start line in the pre-start of a race, whose ends you set yourself; it checks neither land nor depth nor obstacles.

(4) Wind and weather. Wind from open sources comes from weather stations and from a weather model. It arrives with a delay, applies to the station's location or to a coarse grid, and is neither a marine weather forecast nor a gale warning.

(5) Instruments. Etmar shows values from the onboard network (NMEA) as they arrive from the gateway. Etmar cannot tell whether a sensor is miscalibrated. Etmar records the water depth from the depth sounder but does not show it.

(6) Radio procedure cards. To the extent Etmar offers an SOS view, it is a reading aid for the radio call. The call goes out over your marine radio (VHF channel 16, DSC) or by phone, not through Etmar.

(7) Voyage journal. The voyage journal does not replace a ship’s log required by law. Its entries can be changed afterwards.

3 Limits of AIS information, alarms and emergency functions

(1) To the extent Etmar offers alarm and emergency functions, such as an AIS warning or MOB, they help — they are no substitute for a watch or a lookout. They can fail: battery flat, iOS closes the app, no GPS, onboard network down, device out of earshot. The Provider does not promise that an alarm will go off in every situation. In addition, each function has the following limits.

(2) AIS. Etmar records AIS data from the onboard network in the raw recording but currently does not show it. To the extent Etmar shows vessels from AIS data and, for a vessel you tap, the closest point of approach (CPA/TCPA), calculated from its reported position and motion, the following limits apply. To the extent the app offers an AIS warning, it warns when a vessel, on that basis, will come closer than the threshold you set. Limits:

  1. Etmar only sees vessels that transmit AIS. Many craft do not transmit AIS or have it switched off.
  2. If AIS reception drops out, Etmar does not report it. Only before the first AIS signal has arrived does the layers menu show “AIS on — no targets received yet”.
  3. Etmar stops calculating for a vessel from which no new position has arrived for three minutes.
  4. Without a current course of your own, Etmar calculates as if your boat were stationary.
  5. Neither the display nor the warning is a substitute for a lookout or radar. The Collision Regulations apply.

(3) MOB. To the extent the app offers a MOB button, it records position and time when someone presses and holds it, and shows bearing and distance to that point. Limits:

  1. MOB does not watch. Etmar does not detect by itself that someone has gone overboard. The alarm only comes when someone on board holds the button.
  2. Etmar does not send a distress call.
  3. The position is as accurate as the GPS at the moment of triggering. If the last position is too old, Etmar marks the point as provisional or approximate. Etmar does not account for the drift of the person in the water.

(4) To the extent Etmar offers alarm and emergency functions, they are always free. Using Etmar for the first time requires your consent to these terms; to the extent Etmar offers the SOS radio procedure cards (section 2(6)), they can be reached without consent. While your consent to a new text version is pending, alarm and emergency functions, to the extent Etmar offers them, remain usable (section 13).

(2) Anyone using Etmar on board makes sure in particular that

  1. navigation is done with official, corrected charts and the required equipment, with Etmar running alongside;
  2. a lookout and, where the situation calls for it, an anchor watch are kept;
  3. the official marine weather forecast is obtained;
  4. the device is charged, protected from water and within earshot, and Etmar has the permissions it needs (location, notifications);
  5. they know the alarms, to the extent Etmar offers them, before relying on them (for example in demo mode or in harbour), and the thresholds suit their boat and waters;

  7. the crew knows what Etmar does not do, if they rely on Etmar.

(3) When in doubt, assume the less favourable situation, as good seamanship requires.
